4 Brainstorming Techniques with Sticky Notes

2026-11-14 · 7 min read

Few phrases freeze a room like “just throw out some ideas.” Good brainstorming needs rules. The four methods below need nothing but sticky notes, so you can run them as-is on an online canvas. Use just one that fits the situation, or chain them together into an hour-long workshop.

1. Brainwriting — build on ideas in writing instead of speech

When to use it: with a large group, or when only a few people are doing the talking.

How to run it

  1. Give each participant a vertical column on the canvas with their name on it.
  2. For three minutes, everyone adds three idea notes to their own column.
  3. When time is up, everyone moves to the column on their right and adds three notes below that develop the ideas already there.
  4. Repeat until every column has gone all the way around.

Six people produce more than a hundred ideas in thirty minutes. The key is that you “continue” someone else’s idea, which removes the pressure to come up with something brilliant from scratch.

2. Affinity mapping — give scattered ideas a structure

When to use it: when there are plenty of ideas but you can’t see what matters.

Pour all the notes onto one side of the canvas and, without talking, drag similar ones together. It’s fine if someone moves a note another person just placed. When the movement settles, give each cluster a title written as a sentence. “New users can’t find the button” works better than a single word like “UI” — writing a sentence makes the next action visible.

Use a different color for cluster titles so the structure stays clear even after you save the board as a PNG.

3. Crazy 8s — eight sketches in eight minutes

When to use it: when you are making something visual, such as a screen, a poster or a logo.

Make a 2×4 grid of eight rectangles and spend one minute per cell sketching quickly with the pen. Don’t try to draw well. One minute is really short: the first two or three cells will be obvious ideas, but around the fifth cell, directions you would never normally consider start to pop up.

When you’re done, everyone stars their favorite cell and spends thirty seconds explaining why.

4. Dot voting — choose quickly and fairly

When to use it: when you have enough options but can’t decide.

Each person gets three dots and marks them with the pen next to the ideas they like. Putting all three dots on one idea is allowed. The rule is no persuading while voting. Take the two or three ideas with the most dots to the next step.

Dot voting is not the final decision; it is a tool for narrowing what you discuss. If an idea with few dots is truly necessary for a reason, say so now.

Chaining all four

OrderTechniqueTimeResult
1Brainwriting20 minDozens of idea notes
2Affinity mapping15 min5–8 themed clusters
3Dot voting5 min2–3 clusters to focus on
4Crazy 8s15 minConcrete draft sketches

In an hour, a vague topic becomes a draft you can hold in your hands. Move the chosen draft into calendar to-dos and assign an owner.
