Make a Mood Board in 10 Minutes with AI Background Removal

2026-11-14 · 5 min read

A mood board lets you agree on “this kind of feel” with images instead of words. It helps designers, planners and clients picture the same thing. But images simply laid side by side look cluttered because every background is different. Layer images with their backgrounds removed and the board comes together like a single collage.

1. Write the purpose in one line

At the very top of the canvas, write the purpose on a single sticky note — for example “Café rebrand for people in their twenties: warm, but not old-fashioned.” This one line becomes your standard for choosing images. Without it, pretty pictures keep piling up but the direction never settles.

2. Collect images (3 min)

Drag photos you have saved or taken yourself onto the browser window one at a time; each image lands where you drop it. Don’t filter at this stage — just put them in. Fifteen to twenty images is plenty.

Images on a mood board should be ones you took yourself or have permission to use. If the board will be shown publicly, always check sources and licenses.

3. Remove the background from the hero images (3 min)

Press and hold an image with a clear subject — a product, a person, a prop — and AI removes the background. Images without backgrounds look natural even when layered over other images.

  • Works well: photos where subject and background colors clearly differ, and the subject is in focus
  • Harder: blurry photos, and thin details such as hair in colors similar to the background

It also recognizes logos and text images, so you can cleanly cut out an existing brand logo and put it on the board.

4. Arrange in three zones (3 min)

Dividing the board into three zones gives it a story.

  1. Color — images with the color palette you want. Pick sticky-note colors and place two or three representative colors beside them.
  2. Texture and material — tactile feel such as wood, linen or glossy plastic
  3. Scene — spaces and moments where people who use this brand might be

Place the cut-out hero image large in the middle, overlapping all three zones, to draw the eye.

5. Add notes and share (1 min)

Add a short note to each image explaining why you chose it — “this level of saturation”, “this lighting”. With images alone, every viewer focuses on something different. When it is done, invite teammates with an invite link and collect feedback on sticky notes, or save a PNG and send it in your chat.

Checklist

  • Is the one-line purpose at the top?
  • Are there no more than 20 images? (Too many blurs the message.)
  • Are the color, texture and scene zones separated?
  • Does every image say why it was chosen?
  • Did you use only images you have the right to use?
